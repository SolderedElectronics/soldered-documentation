Every part in the box, what it does, and what to watch for when you push it in. Where a part has its own documentation, it is linked.

This page is for looking things up. You do not need to read it before starting.

## The board and the breadboard

### NULA DeepSleep ESP32-S3

The board that runs everything. An ESP32-S3 with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, a USB-C port for programming and power, a Qwiic socket, an RGB LED and a real-time clock. Male headers are fitted, so it pushes straight into the breadboard.

Deep-sleep current is around 7 µA with the onboard LED jumper opened, which is why this board is the one in the kit.

### Large breadboard

A block of holes wired together underneath in a fixed pattern. Lay it down so the numbers run left to right: columns are numbered 1 to 63, rows are lettered a to j. Each column holds five joined holes above the channel and five below, and the four long rows along the top and bottom edges carry power the whole width of the board.

<WarningBox>**One hole holds one thing.** A wire end and a component leg never share a hole. If two things need to be connected, put them in the same column, not the same hole.</WarningBox>

{/* SHOT c-breadboard: the kit's breadboard on white, lying down, power rows and channel clear */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/breadboard.webp" alt="The breadboard from the kit" caption="The breadboard. Power rows along the top and bottom, a channel across the middle" width="620px"/>

### Jumper wire set

Short stiff wires for making connections. Colour means nothing to the circuit, but it means a lot to you. Use one colour per connection and a photograph of your board stays readable.

## Passive parts

### 330 Ω resistor, 25 of them

Bands orange, orange, brown. Limits the current through an LED. One per LED, never shared.

An LED cannot limit its own current, so without a resistor the pin supplies as much as it can until the LED is damaged. Chapter 4 measures exactly what this resistor does, and tells you what your LED actually wants.

{/* SHOT c-330: one 330 ohm resistor close up, bands orange orange brown legible, an existing Soldered photo, cut out; replace with the kit's own part */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/resistor-330.webp" alt="A 330 ohm resistor with its colour bands" caption="330 Ω: orange, orange, brown" width="220px"/>

### 10 kΩ resistor, 15 of them

Bands brown, black, orange. Used as a pull-up for a button, as the fixed half of a divider with the photoresistor, as the charging resistor in the RC circuit, and as the base resistor for a transistor. Four different jobs in one value.

{/* SHOT c-10k: one 10 k resistor close up, bands brown black orange legible, an existing Soldered photo, cut out; replace with the kit's own part */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/resistor-10k.webp" alt="A 10 k resistor with its colour bands" caption="10 kΩ: brown, black, orange" width="110px"/>

### 10 kΩ potentiometer, 3 of them

A resistor with a sliding contact. The two outer legs are the ends of a fixed track and the middle leg, the **wiper**, touches it somewhere between them. Wired across the supply it turns a knob into a voltage.

Legs are on a 5 mm pitch, so they land in alternate columns rather than adjacent ones.

{/* SHOT c-pot: one potentiometer from above and from the side, the three legs and the 5 mm pitch visible */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/potentiometer.webp" alt="A 10 k potentiometer with its shaft and three legs" caption="The potentiometer. The middle leg is the wiper" width="150px"/>

### 100 nF capacitor, 5 of them

Small yellow disc, no polarity, either way round. Two jobs in this kit: the fast half of the RC measurement, and decoupling, which means sitting right next to a chip's supply pins to absorb the current spike when its outputs all switch at once.

{/* SHOT c-100nf: one ceramic capacitor close up, the 104 marking legible */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/ceramic-100nf.webp" alt="A 100 nF ceramic capacitor" caption="100 nF, marked 104. No polarity, so either way round" width="115px"/>

### 10 µF electrolytic capacitor, 5 of them

Small cylinder with a stripe down one side. **Polarised**: the leg next to the stripe is negative and goes to ground.

<WarningBox>**Fitting the 10 µF backwards is one of the very few ways to damage a part in this kit.** The stripe goes to ground, every time.</WarningBox>

{/* SHOT c-10uf: one electrolytic close up, the stripe and the shorter leg both visible */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/airplane-solder-kit/capacitor-marked.jpg" alt="An electrolytic capacitor with its legs marked" caption="An electrolytic capacitor. The stripe marks the negative leg" width="340px"/>

## Things that light up and make noise

### 5 mm LED, 13 of them

Three each of red, yellow, green and blue, plus one RGB. Coloured domes with two legs. The **long leg** is the anode and goes towards the positive side; the short leg sits next to a flat spot on the rim. Lights in one direction only, and always through its own 330 Ω resistor.

Each colour needs a different voltage to light, so a blue LED looks dim in a circuit built around a red one. Chapter 4 measures yours.

The RGB one has **four legs**: one per colour, plus a common. It needs three resistors and three PWM pins. The manual drives the WS2812B already on the board instead, which needs one pin and no resistors, so this four-legged LED is spare and yours to experiment with.

{/* SHOT c-led: several 5 mm LEDs of different colours, one turned so the long leg and the flat spot show */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/leds.webp" alt="Several 5 mm LEDs in different colours" caption="5 mm LEDs. The long leg is the anode" width="230px"/>

### Buzzer QMB-09B-03

A **passive** buzzer: a coil inside pulls on a steel disc, and the disc springs back when the coil lets go. It makes no sound at all from a steady voltage, and the frequency of the signal you give it is the pitch.

Its legs are very short and one column apart, so plan where everything lands before pushing it in. It draws more than a pin should supply, so it is driven through a transistor.

## Things that sense

### 10 kΩ photoresistor, 5 of them

A resistor whose value falls as light hits it. Two legs, no polarity. In the dark it reaches hundreds of kilohms; under a bright light it drops to a few hundred ohms.

The board cannot measure resistance, only voltage, so it is always paired with a fixed resistor to make a divider.

{/* SHOT c-ldr: one photoresistor close up, the zigzag track on its face in focus */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/photoresistor.webp" alt="A photoresistor with its zigzag sensing track" caption="The photoresistor. The zigzag is the part that changes" width="150px"/>

### Small THT pushbutton, 5 of them

Four legs in two pairs. The two legs on the same side of the body are permanently joined to each other, and pressing joins one pair to the other.

<WarningBox>**A pushbutton has an orientation.** Fitted 90° out, the two legs you wired are a pair that is already connected, so the pin reads pressed all the time. Push it in across the centre channel and each pair lands on its own side.</WarningBox>

{/* SHOT c-button: one pushbutton from above and from the side, all four legs visible */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/pushbutton.webp" alt="A 6 mm tactile pushbutton with its four legs" caption="The pushbutton. Four legs in two pairs, and the two on the same side are joined" width="100px"/>

### TMP117 temperature sensor

A precision temperature sensor on a Qwiic board. No wiring, one cable. It can also watch the temperature on its own and raise a flag when it leaves a band, which is what makes it useful to a board that spends its time asleep.

## Things that switch and move

### NPN transistor, TO-92, 5 of them

Lets a small current at the base control a much larger one from collector to emitter. Used as a switch for the buzzer and as the subject of its own chapter.

<WarningBox>**Check the leg order against your own part.** TO-92 legs are usually emitter, base, collector from left to right with the flat face towards you, but this varies by part number. Getting it wrong costs you a transistor.</WarningBox>

{/* SHOT c-transistor: one TO-92 close up, flat face towards the camera, the part number legible and all three legs in frame */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/transistor.webp" alt="A TO-92 transistor, flat face towards you, with its part number legible" caption="The transistor. The number printed on the flat face is what tells you the leg order" width="75px"/>

### 1N4148 diode, 5 of them

Conducts one way and blocks the other. The **band** marks the cathode, the end current flows out of. Used to measure what a diode actually does, and as flyback protection across the buzzer.

{/* SHOT c-diode: one 1N4148 close up, the band at one end legible */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/lofi-noise-kit/diode_anode_cathode.png" alt="A diode with its anode and cathode ends marked" caption="The band marks the cathode, the end current leaves by" width="280px"/>

### SN74HC595N shift register

A 16-pin chip that turns a sequence of bits into eight parallel outputs, so three pins drive eight LEDs. Orient it by the **notch** in the end of the package, not by any round dimple, which on this part is not at the pin 1 end.

It needs two things that give no obvious sign when they are missing: its master reset held high, and a 100 nF right at its supply pins.

{/* SHOT c-595: the DIP chip alone, notch and part number legible */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/shift-register.webp" alt="An SN74HC595N shift register in a 16-pin DIP package" caption="The notch marks the pin 1 end" width="215px"/>

### SG90 servo

A motor, a gearbox, a position sensor and a controller in one box. You tell it where to be, not how fast to turn, using the width of a pulse repeated every 20 ms.

<WarningBox>**The servo's power comes from `VCC`, never from `3V3`.** `3V3` is a small regulator meant to run the chip, and starting a motor from it browns the board out.</WarningBox>

<InfoBox>The kit has no bracket, tape or glue, so there is nothing to mount on the servo horn and no example asks you to.</InfoBox>

{/* SHOT c-servo: the SG90 with its lead and horns, wire colours legible */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/nula-inventor-kit/servo.webp" alt="An SG90 servo with its three-wire lead and horns" caption="Orange is signal, red is power, brown is ground" width="270px"/>

### 1-channel relay

A mechanical switch operated by an electromagnet, on a Qwiic board, with the coil driving and the isolation done for you. It is slow, it is audible, and it has a finite number of operations in it. That is why the examples work hard to switch it as rarely as possible.

## Connections

### Qwiic cable, 10 cm, 5 of them

Four wires carrying power, ground and the two lines of an I2C bus. Every Qwiic module has two sockets, so modules chain, and the connector only fits one way.

<InfoBox>A Qwiic cable carries those four wires and nothing else. Any extra pin a module offers, such as an alert output, is on a pad you would have to wire yourself.</InfoBox>

{/* SHOT c-qwiic: one Qwiic cable, both plugs in shot, using an existing Soldered photo, replace with the kit's own part */}
<CenteredImage src="/img/easyc_transparent.png" alt="A 10 cm Qwiic cable with a plug at each end" caption="Four wires, and the plug only fits one way" width="430px"/>

### USB-C cable

Supplied with the kit. Programming and power over one lead. It is a data cable. A charge-only one gives you a board that never appears as a port, which is worth knowing if you ever reach for a different cable.

## What is not in the box

No battery, and nothing here needs one. No meter either. The board measures its own circuits instead, and that is the idea the manual is built on.
